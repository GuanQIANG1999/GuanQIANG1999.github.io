Clinical Risk Prediction & Healthcare Data Analysis

Applied Machine Learning Researcher · Mitacs Accelerate · Jan 2026 – Present

Western University / CAMH / Kelello Health, Canada

The problem

Clinical risk prediction requires extracting useful evidence from incomplete structured records and unstructured notes. I built an end-to-end pipeline covering data preparation, clinical text analysis, feature extraction, model training, and evaluation across three risk categories.

My contribution

  • Processed 12,841 emergency department records, including 7,924 labeled encounters, for clinical risk modeling.
  • Combined clinical-note features with structured patient data, evaluating Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, TF–IDF text models, and multimodal fusion.
  • Addressed class imbalance and missing clinical evidence; compared the multimodal approach with a structured-data XGBoost baseline.
  • Built reproducible workflows with Python, scikit-learn, XGBoost, PostgreSQL, and MLflow in a secure HPC environment, with reusable NLP and evaluation components for future transformer integration.

Engineering relevance. Connects clinical data processing, multimodal modeling, and reproducible evaluation in the intended secure computing environment.

Results & evaluation

12,841Emergency department records; 7,924 labeled encounters
66.3%Classification accuracy; structured baseline 57.3%
0.628Macro-F1; structured baseline 0.461

Three-category clinical risk prediction. Relative Macro-F1 improvement is approximately 36% over the structured-data XGBoost baseline. This clinical project evaluation is separate from the CRS-Triage paper benchmark below.

Additional longitudinal analysis: 19,323 GAD-7 and 25,757 PHQ-9 assessments, identifying 901 and 1,481 follow-up pairs respectively.

Focus: Clinical NLP, multimodal learning, scikit-learn, XGBoost, PostgreSQL, MLflow, HPC

Selected engineering project · Document AI

Deep Learning-Based Mathematical OCR on NVIDIA Jetson

2021

Mathematical expressions in scanned documents require recognizing both symbols and their spatial structure. I built an end-to-end system that detects formulas and converts them into editable LaTeX.

My contribution

  • Integrated Python, OpenCV, PyTorch, and YOLOv5 for formula detection, preprocessing, sequence recognition, and LaTeX generation.
  • Implemented a CNN–Transformer recognizer for symbol and mathematical-structure recognition.
  • Deployed inference on NVIDIA Jetson Nano with Raspberry Pi image acquisition.
  • Used TensorRT and FP16 to optimize embedded inference, and connected localization with structured expression generation and editable export.

Engineering relevance. Connects visual recognition with embedded deployment and reusable, editable mathematical output.

Results & evaluation

94.2%Formula detection mAP@0.5
86.5%Expression-level LaTeX exact match
1.15 sLatency per image; 1.74 s → 1.15 s

Symbol-recognition accuracy was 96.1%. TensorRT and FP16 optimization reduced end-to-end latency by 33.9%, from 1.74 to 1.15 seconds per image. Detection mAP, symbol accuracy, and expression exact match are separate measures.

Focus: Python, OpenCV, PyTorch, YOLOv5, CNN–Transformer, TensorRT, FP16, Jetson Nano
